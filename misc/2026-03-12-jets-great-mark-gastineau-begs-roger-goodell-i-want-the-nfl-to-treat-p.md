# Jets Great Mark Gastineau Begs Roger Goodell: 'I Want the NFL to Treat People Right'

Former New York Jet Mark Gastineau is begging NFL commissioner Roger Goodell to take care of his players.

Gastineau spoke out in an emotional interview with WOR Radio's Pete McCarthy last Thursday. The five-time Pro Bowler previously revealed that he's been diagnosed with Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease, and has suggested that injuries suffered over his NFL career are largely to blame.

"I want the NFL to treat people right," Gastineau told McCarthy on Thursday. "I want to hold you to your promise, Roger Goodell. You said, 'Anything I need!' ... I want the players to be treated right."

Gastineau played for the Jets over nine seasons, racking up 107.5 sacks between 1979 and 1988 and becoming one of the best known (and most feared) players in the league. According to the interview, the defensive end also befriended a young Goodell, in the days the future commissioner was just a public relations intern for the Jets.

Concussion-Related Health Issues

Are concussions really tied to Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease? A 2016 study found that people who'd sustained head injuries were 3.5 times more likely to develop Parkinson's Disease than people who hadn't, but it did not find a link between head injuries and an increased risk of Alzheimer's disease. However, in 2017, different researchers found that concussions accelerated Alzheimer's-related symptoms in people who had a genetic risk for the disease.

(Guys: If you're concerned about your own brain health, always pay attention to the earliest sign of Alzheimer's, and keep in mind that consistent diet and exercise has been shown to stave off dementia and Parkinson's later in life.)

In January of last year, the NFL issued a massive, $1 billion settlement to more than 20,000 former players suing the organization over concussion-related health issues. Gastineau claimed during the interview that he is listed as a plaintiff in the settlement, but has yet to see any monetary compensation. (In November, the Washington Post reported that most players still hadn't "seen a dime" from the settlement.)

The retired pass-rusher — who was accompanied by his wife and lawyer — grew emotional describing the everyday struggles he faces due to his injuries.
