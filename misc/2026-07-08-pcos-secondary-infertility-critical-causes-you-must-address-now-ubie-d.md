Secondary infertility in people with PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome) is often caused by hormonal imbalances, irregular or absent ovulation, insulin resistance, or related health conditions such as thyroid disorders and obesity. Even after a previous successful pregnancy, these factors can make conceiving again more difficult.

Secondary infertility means having trouble getting pregnant again after having a baby before. For people with PCOS, this can happen for several reasons.

Causes of Secondary Infertility with PCOS:

Hormonal Imbalances: PCOS often causes hormone levels to be out of balance, which can make it hard for the body to release eggs regularly, a process called ovulation.

Ovulation Issues: Because of these hormone changes, ovulation might not happen every month, making it harder to get pregnant.

Other Health Conditions: Sometimes, other health issues can also affect fertility. These can include being overweight or having insulin resistance, which are more common in people with PCOS.
