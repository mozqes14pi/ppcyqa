“I think the dope thing about the Nineties collection—hold on for a second,” says Kevin Hart, actor and comedian, from his home in Los Angles. “It’s all over the floor,” his wife, Eniko, says, and almost on cue, there’s a bark in the background. “Jesus Christ,” Hart says. “My dog just opened up a quinoa packet.”

Hart, 42, is a man as known for his humor as he is for his tireless energy, which manifests itself in multiple movies and TV shows every year, comedy sets around the country, a nascent music career under the name “Chocolate Droppa,” and about half a dozen other projects. His 2020 special, Zero F*cks Given, is currently nominated for a Grammy, and his recent Netflix crime drama True Story smashed streaming records. But the real reason he’s talking to Men’s Health today is to promote his longstanding collaboration with the athleisure line Fabletics, which releases the third iteration of his signature Hart Collection today.

[Nineties-inspired Athleisure Collection](https://collagen-atelier.bbsxyxy.com/blog/0600960332.html)

Designed by Hart and Eniko, the Hart Collection features men’s and women’s workout tops and bottoms as well as lifestyle pieces like sweats and knit beanies. For those who have followed Hart’s last decade, the release just makes sense: Passionate about his fitness, the Philly native has hosted group runs in cities he tours while rubbing shoulders with professional athletes and Olympians regularly. “I eat it, I breathe it, I sleep it, and it’s never not going to be that,” he says. “It’s a long-term investment and one where I can always get great return.”

But the Hart Collection is more than just core workout staples in grey and blacks; this third edition shows that Hart has his feet under him and is starting to stretch. The husband-and-wife team filled an idea board with photos of LL Cool J, Run DMC, and other hip-hop celebrities that defined the Nineties, and its pieces feature bold colorblocking and prominent seams that make a statement. “There was a high level of fun and a high level of cool that people looked for and that people wanted,” he says of the epoch. “It was, 'How do we pay homage to a time that was so, so memorable?’”

From silhouettes to function to, frankly, each piece’s affordability, the Harts’ goal was to surprise. “We want to keep you on your toes,” he says. “We hit you with some curve balls that you don’t expect.” But it ultimately comes down to comfort, crafting pieces you don’t want to take off. “I’m a hoodie fanatic. I can’t put them down,” Hart says of his favorite of the myriad he designed. “I’m in it all day, every day.”

Working for 12 months alongside your significant other and bickering over minutiae might be too much for some guys, but Hart has nothing but good things to say about the process. “I love that we’re able to really put ourselves in a creative hub to ping-pong off each other,” he says. “There are no bad ideas. She’s been a great asset.”

While Hart continues to prioritize his own fitness from his home gym, hitting workouts six or seven days a week, with the new year has come a new focus on recovery. “What I’m falling in love with now is just taking care of myself differently,” he says.“You get to a point where you realize you’re getting older, and as you get older you’ve got to prioritize self-care.” Recent additions include massage, acupuncture, and sleep. “At one point it was all about, go, go, go,” he continues, “but now it becomes, ‘How am I going to last?’”

All of this—his comedy, his content, the Hart Collection through Fabletics, or even the dog spilling quinoa across the floor—clamors for time, and Hart can sympathize with guys who think they’re just too busy to put the time in to achieve their fitness goals. But that doesn’t mean he agrees. “Look, there’s time for everything that you want to make time for,” he concludes. “For me, the things that I found work the best is just prioritizing. Give yourself the time. It all starts with yourself.”
