Heavy Lifting: Red Light Therapy for Joint Protection in Strength Athletes

Heavy lifting places extraordinary stress on joints, connective tissues and muscles. Strength athletes—especially powerlifters—are always seeking safe, effective ways to boost recovery, maintain joint health and stay on top of their training. One promising tool is red light therapy (RLT), sometimes called photobiomodulation. Below, we'll explain how RLT works, review the evidence, and share practical guidelines for using RLT to support joint health in powerlifting.

What the Research Says

Several studies have explored red and near-infrared light for joint health and exercise performance. Key findings include:

A 2016 meta-analysis published in the Journal of Photomedicine & Photobiology reported that photobiomodulation significantly reduces delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) and improves muscle performance.

Research on osteoarthritis patients has shown improvements in pain and function after RLT, suggesting cartilage-protective effects.

A 2018 trial in trained athletes found that pre-exercise RLT improved muscle strength and reduced markers of muscle damage when compared to placebo light.

Although many studies focus on general muscle recovery, growing evidence supports the specific use of "red light therapy for powerlifting and joint health." While more large-scale trials in elite lifters are needed, existing data are promising.

Practical Guidelines for Using RLT in Powerlifting

To maximize joint protection and recovery, follow these evidence-based recommendations:

Select Appropriate Wavelengths

Red light (630–680 nm) for superficial tissues (skin, tendons).

Near-infrared (800–940 nm) for deeper penetration (muscles, joints).

Target Key Areas

Knees, hips, shoulders and elbows—common stress points in powerlifting.

Apply light directly over tendons and joint lines.

Dose and Duration

Energy density: 4–10 J/cm² per session at each site.

Treatment time: 5–15 minutes per area, depending on device power.

Frequency: 2–5 times per week for maintenance or acute recovery.

Distance and Positioning

Keep the device 1–5 cm from the skin, following manufacturer guidelines.

Ensure even coverage; move the device slowly to avoid hotspots.

Integration with Training

Use RLT pre-workout to warm up tissues and improve blood flow.

Apply post-workout to speed recovery and reduce inflammation.

Consistency Is Key

Benefits accumulate over multiple sessions.

Track pain, mobility and performance to adjust timing and dosage.

Red light therapy is generally safe when used properly. However, keep these points in mind:

Avoid looking directly into powerful near-infrared LEDs. Some devices include goggles for eye protection.

Never use RLT over areas with active cancer, as increased cell activity could be contraindicated.

If you have photosensitivity disorders or take photosensitizing medication, consult a healthcare professional first.

Always follow the manufacturer's instructions for your device.
