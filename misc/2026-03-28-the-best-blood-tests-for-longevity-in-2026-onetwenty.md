Four things separate a test you act on from a number you forget. We weighed each service against all four.

Depth. It should include biomarkers with strong evidence for risk stratification and prevention, not just the basic lipid and metabolic panel your doctor already runs.

Integration. Blood is one input. The clearest picture comes from combining it with continuous data from your wearables, scale, and blood pressure cuff in one place.

Common questions

Longevity testing, answered.

Which biomarkers matter most for longevity?

Beyond the standard lipid panel and CBC, markers worth tracking include ApoB and Lp(a) for cardiovascular risk, hs-CRP for inflammation, fasting insulin and HbA1c for metabolic health, and homocysteine. A good longevity panel also covers hormones, thyroid, liver, kidney, and key nutrients. These are risk indicators rather than definitive predictors, so they are most useful interpreted alongside your history by a clinician.

How often should I test?

It depends on your health status, cardiovascular risk, medications, and goals rather than a single rule. There is no evidence-based guideline recommending quarterly testing for healthy adults, but people actively monitoring a lifestyle change, a medication, or a therapy may benefit from testing more often than the once-a-year screening most others get. Set the interval with your clinician.

Are these covered by insurance?

Mostly no. These are out-of-pocket memberships rather than insurance-billed care, though many are HSA and FSA eligible, which can offset the cost.

Can I combine blood work with my wearable and scale data?

With most services on this list, not really. OneTwenty is the exception: your wearable, scale, and blood pressure data are unified with your labs in a single profile, so the blood work is read in the context of how you actually live day to day.
