# A New Approach to Kicking Nicotine Pouches

PROGRESS IN DRIVING down tobacco use in the U.S. is facing a new, discreet threat. Whether it’s before sets at the gym, between meetings or classes, or during a late night study session, nicotine pouches are becoming more popular, particularly among younger men and teens. Sales of these small packets of nicotine powder and flavorings like mint, citrus, and cinnamon are one of the fastest growing tobacco or nicotine product categories in the United States—nearly tripling from $145 million to $404 million between January 2023 and December 2024. And concerning trends are emerging among young people related to the use of these “lip pillows" or "upper deckies" as they’re often referred to because of where they are placed between the lip and gum. For example, recently released federal data shows that use among 12th graders more than doubled between 2023 and 2025.

Nicotine is highly addictive and taste aside, the real appeal for Gen Z might be the hit of nicotine that floods the brain with dopamine, a feel-good hormone that’s released after activities like exercise and sex.

The problem with high-dose exposure is that the dopamine rush “wears off very, very fast,” says Megan Jacobs, senior vice president of product at Truth Initiative, a nonprofit organization committed to reducing nicotine dependence in young adults. “As a result, the brain can start to crave more nicotine to replace that dopamine rush it got from the last time you used it.” Over time, developing tolerance to nicotine can lead to higher doses and more frequent use necessary to get the same rush, leading to dependence and difficulty quitting.

WHILE NICOTINE CAN be harmful to anyone, young people can be particularly vulnerable to its effects. Research shows that exposure during adolescence can disrupt brain circuits involved in attention, learning, and impulse control—changes that may make it harder to regulate habits and increase the risk of substance dependence later in life.

And yet, nicotine pouches have gained a reputation as clean, safe alternatives to vaping and smoking, in part because they aren’t burned or inhaled like traditional cigarettes, according to Jacobs. “Nicotine pouches may be perceived as harmless,” she says, “and many people might not be aware of the risks that come from them.”

It doesn’t help matters that these products are particularly popular online—not just in hyper-masculine communities like MMA forums and sports podcasts, but on social media, where users talk about “spiraling” (using multiple packets at once) and turning to the pouches to cope with stress or boredom. “They’ve really become a cultural phenomenon, especially in online spaces popular with men,” Jacobs says.

Five Nicotine Swaps That Work

HERE ARE THE a few common reasons young men use nicotine products—and what to do instead:

For a pre-workout boost

Athletes and weekend warriors may turn to nicotine pouches and vapes for a perceived jolt of energy before exercise. But research has shown that nicotine has little, if any, effect on muscular strength, power, or endurance. A safer option: Eat a banana pre-workout, as simple carbohydrates before an endurance event can improve performance.

For a buzz without the burn

Nicotine delivers a quick spike in stimulation, which is part of the appeal, but the effect is short-lived. For a similar jolt without the crash, try a short burst of intense exercise, like 30 to 60 seconds of fast stair climbing or bodyweight squats. High-intensity movement can increase alertness and dopamine, delivering a quick “buzz” effect without risking nicotine dependence.

For better focus

Learning something new? Don’t reach for a nicotine pouch to sharpen your focus. Research shows that we can learn skills more effectively by taking short breaks during practice. That study found that when right-handed people tried to type with their left hand, they were more successful after they had short periods of “waking rest”—time where they allowed their brains to solidify the memory of the task.

For relaxation

Tobacco products—including nicotine vapes, pouches, and cigarettes—are often touted as a stress-reliever. But people who smoke are more likely to have feelings of anxiety or depression than people who don’t. Instead of reaching for a smoke or vape to deal with anxiety, pull up a funny video on your phone. Laughter has been shown to lower stress by suppressing the levels of the stress hormone cortisol and boosting dopamine, according to a 2021 review.

For more confidence

Feeling nervous? Nicotine pouches or e-cigarettes may provide a brief sense of ease, but they can leave you feeling jittery later on. A better alternative is preparation-based confidence: Take a minute to mentally rehearse what you’re about to do. Research shows that mentally running through a task can improve execution and decision-making, which naturally builds confidence—without the rebound effects of nicotine.

A Smarter Way to Outsmart Nicotine

THE BEST TIME to quit using nicotine is now. That’s why Truth Initiative, in partnership with the Mayo Clinic, has developed EX Program: a quitting plan that helps people stop using cigarettes and other tobacco products, including nicotine pouches. The program, which has helped millions of people, can boost the odds of quitting by up to 40 percent.

“EX Program helps you understand what’s happening in your own brain,” Jacobs says. “It helps you identify those moments where you’re craving nicotine, or feeling triggered to use tobacco products, and guides you through making it through those tough times without nicotine.”

Quitting nicotine pouches can be different than trying to kick a cigarette addiction, Jacobs says. “There are some unique strategies and specific ways that we tailor the guidance and advice that we give to people who are quitting pouches,” she explains. “The feeling of using a nicotine pouch is different from the feeling of smoking, and the social settings where people find themselves using it are different.”
