Why Your Doctor Monitors Androgens in Women Taking Shilajit

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Shilajit may boost steroidogenesis in women, increasing testosterone, DHEA-S, and androstenedione levels. Without proper monitoring, this hormonal shift can trigger side effects such as hirsutism, acne, and menstrual irregularities, while also raising long-term metabolic health risks. Routine lab testing helps guide safe dosage adjustments and early intervention.

Explanation

Shilajit is a mineral-rich resin used in traditional Ayurvedic medicine to support energy, cognitive function, and sexual health. In recent years, some women have turned to Shilajit supplements hoping to boost vitality—and even testosterone levels—naturally. While Shilajit and testosterone for women might sound like a promising combo, it's important to understand why doctors carefully track androgen levels (male-type hormones) when women take this supplement.

What Are Androgens—and Why They Matter in Women

Androgens are hormones best known for their role in male development, but they're also essential in women for:

Bone density

Muscle mass

Libido and sexual function

Mood regulation

Energy balance

Monitoring helps protect fertility and endocrine balance.

Avoiding Long-Term Health Risks

Prolonged hyperandrogenism can raise insulin resistance and cardiovascular risk.

Tracking hormones supports overall metabolic health.

Individual Variations in Response

What to Expect: The Monitoring Process

Your doctor's plan typically involves:

Baseline Testing

Blood tests for total/free testosterone, DHEA-S, androstenedione

Comprehensive metabolic panel (liver, kidney function)

Lipid profile and blood sugar

Supplement Initiation

Starting Shilajit at a low dose (as per reputable product guidelines)

Documenting symptoms: energy, mood, menstrual changes, skin/hair status

Follow-Up Labs (4–12 weeks)

Rechecking androgen levels to ensure they remain within the reference range for women

Adjusting dose or discontinuing based on results and symptom report

Ongoing Surveillance (Every 3–6 months)

Yearly or semi-annual panels if you continue Shilajit long-term

Periodic symptom reviews to catch late-onset changes

When to Seek Further Evaluation

If hormone tests show elevated androgens despite dose adjustments, your doctor may explore:

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)

Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH)

Adrenal or ovarian tumors (rare)

Thyroid disorders

Appropriate imaging studies or specialist referrals ensure no serious underlying condition is missed.
