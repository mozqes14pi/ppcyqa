Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

NAD+ is a coenzyme that supports energy production, DNA repair, and cellular health in the brain. Early studies and anecdotal reports suggest NAD+ may help improve focus in people with ADHD, but high-quality clinical evidence remains limited. Before trying NAD+ supplementation, consult your doctor to weigh potential benefits, safety concerns, drug interactions, and the appropriate form and dosage for your needs.

Because ADHD symptoms can overlap with other conditions—like anxiety, sleep disorders, or thyroid issues—it's important to understand what's actually driving your focus challenges before adding a supplement. Explanation

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) can make it hard to maintain focus, manage impulsivity, and stay organized. Recently, some people have looked into NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) as a way to support brain health and improve attention. If you'd like to explore whether NAD+ might help with your ADHD focus, here's how to prepare for and guide that conversation with your doctor.

What Is NAD+?

NAD+ is a coenzyme found in every cell of your body. It plays key roles in:

Energy production: Helps convert food into ATP, the energy currency of cells.

DNA repair: Supports enzymes that maintain healthy DNA.

Cellular health: Activates sirtuins, proteins involved in aging and inflammation.

Because the brain uses lots of energy and relies on healthy cell function, NAD+ has become a topic of interest for cognitive health and focus.

Theoretical Link Between NAD+ and ADHD Focus

While ADHD is primarily linked to differences in neurotransmitters (like dopamine and norepinephrine), there's a theory that boosting cellular energy and resilience could indirectly support attention:

Improved mitochondrial function may enhance overall brain energy.

Better DNA repair and reduced oxidative stress might help neurons function optimally.

Sirtuin activation could support neuroplasticity—the brain's ability to adapt and form new connections.

However, it's important to stress that this is an emerging area. Most studies so far are in cells or animal models, and high-quality clinical trials in people with ADHD are limited.

What the Research Says

Current evidence on NAD+ and ADHD focus is sparse:

Preclinical studies: Show NAD+ precursors can protect neurons and boost energy metabolism.

Small-scale human trials: Have examined NAD+ for mood, fatigue, or general cognition, but not specifically for ADHD.

Anecdotal reports: Some individuals report feeling more alert or focused, but these experiences are not the same as controlled clinical data.

Your doctor can help you weigh the potential benefits against the uncertainty and decide if exploring NAD+ makes sense for your situation.

Safety and Risks of NAD+ Supplementation

Before asking about NAD+, consider the common forms and their safety profiles:

Oral supplements (e.g., nicotinamide riboside, nicotinamide mononucleotide): Generally well tolerated, but long-term safety in ADHD populations is unknown.

Intravenous (IV) NAD+: More invasive, carries risks like infection, vein irritation, or allergic reactions.

Drug interactions: NAD+ precursors may interact with medications you're already taking.

Discussing these factors with your doctor ensures you make an informed choice.

Next Steps After Your Appointment

After talking with your doctor:

Review your doctor's recommendations:

If you decide to try NAD+, confirm the form, dose, and duration.

Ask about scheduling follow-up visits and any necessary lab tests.

Keep a treatment journal:

Note daily doses, any side effects, and changes in focus.

Use simple scales (e.g., 1–5) to rate attention, energy, and mood.

Stay in touch with your doctor:

Report any adverse effects promptly.

Discuss whether to adjust the dose or switch strategies based on your progress.

Evaluate other focus-support strategies:

Behavioral therapy, coaching, or structured routines.

Diet, exercise, sleep hygiene, and mindfulness practices.

Using NAD+ doesn't replace proven ADHD treatments; it may be one part of a bigger plan.

Balancing Hope with Realism

It's natural to look for new ways to improve your focus. NAD+ shows promise in supporting brain health, but the evidence for ADHD-specific benefits is still emerging. By asking the right questions and working closely with your doctor, you'll be better positioned to decide whether NAD+ makes sense for you.

When to Seek Immediate Medical Advice

While exploring NAD+ is generally low-risk, always take symptoms seriously. Speak to a doctor right away if you experience:

Severe side effects (e.g., chest pain, difficulty breathing, sudden confusion)

Worsening mood or thoughts of self-harm

Any symptom that feels life-threatening or out of control

Your healthcare team is your partner in safety and well-being.

Discussing NAD+ and ADHD focus with your doctor empowers you to make informed choices.
