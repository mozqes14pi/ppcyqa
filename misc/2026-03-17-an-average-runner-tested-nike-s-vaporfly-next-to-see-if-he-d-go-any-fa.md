"They do feel springy when you put them on and stand up, you feel like you're getting pushed forward the whole way around," he says. "They're a lot lighter, I'll tell you that... The uphill, I feel like I'm getting pushed up it a bit more. There's more energy returned in the heel going forwards, and it's not as hard."

"The heel is nice and cushioned compared to my other shoes, but do I feel faster?" He continues. "In terms of my body, my feet and my legs feel a lot more protected and I can feel the benefit there, but ultimately as the average runner, I think my aerobic fitness is letting me down, and I don't think that's something the shoes can help with."

After finishing the second 5k, Tom feels better than he did during the first run: "The muscles in my body feel like they could go on, and that might be down to the added support," he says. "My feet feel nicer, and they are quite a tight feet, so it's almost like having a compression sock on."

Tom completes his 5k in the Nikes with a time of 31:54. That's an improvement of 2 minutes 41 seconds, 7.8 percent faster than his original running time, and Tom credits that with the carbon plate and energy return in the shoe's design.

"They're comfy, and if you've got the dollar, they look cool too," he says. "But ultimately... you can't cheat aerobic fitness with shoes."
