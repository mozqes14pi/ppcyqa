What Is a Coffee Enema?

A coffee enema involves inserting a mixture of brewed coffee and water into the rectum via the colon. Promoters claim this delivers caffeine directly to the liver through the portal vein, stimulating bile flow and antioxidant activity (such as glutathione release).

Key points:

Typically uses 1–2 tablespoons of coffee in 1–2 quarts of water.

Retained for about 10–15 minutes.

Practiced at home or in alternative therapy clinics.

Why People Try Coffee Enemas

Advocates suggest coffee enemas can:

Improve liver detoxification.

Relieve constipation.

Boost energy and mental clarity.

Support overall "cleansing" regimens.

Yet, credible scientific evidence for these benefits is very limited. Major medical organizations do not endorse coffee enemas for liver health or any medical condition.

Safer Alternatives for Supporting Liver Health

Instead of coffee enemas, consider evidence-based strategies:

Balanced DietEat plenty of fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins.

Precautions If You Still Consider a Coffee Enema

Even if you decide to proceed, take these safety steps:

Use sterile, food-grade equipment.

Brew coffee with filtered water; avoid additives.

Keep solutions at body temperature (98–100°F).

Limit frequency to no more than once per week.

Stop immediately if you feel pain, dizziness, or fever.
