Healing can sometimes feel like it's taking forever. If you're dealing with a cut, sprain, surgery recovery or chronic wound that just won't close up, you're not alone—and there are real reasons why your body may need a helping hand.

Common Reasons for Slow Healing

Age• Cell turnover slows down as we get older.• Skin and blood vessels become less elastic, reducing nutrient flow.

Nutrition• Protein, vitamins (especially A and C), zinc and iron are essential building blocks.• Deficiencies can delay collagen production and tissue repair.

Blood Flow• Poor circulation (from diabetes, smoking or vascular disease) limits oxygen and nutrient delivery.• Areas with less blood supply—like elbows, ankles or the heart of a large wound—heal more slowly.

Medical Conditions• Diabetes, autoimmune diseases and chronic infections can all impair immune response.• Medications such as steroids or chemotherapy agents can also suppress healing.

Lifestyle Factors• Smoking constricts blood vessels and impairs immune cells.• Stress can raise cortisol levels, which slows down tissue regeneration.• Lack of sleep interrupts growth hormone release, vital for repair.

Infection and Inflammation• Ongoing infection consumes immune resources and damages healthy tissue.• Chronic inflammation, as seen in rheumatoid arthritis or eczema, disrupts normal repair phases.

If any of these apply to you, your next steps should include a thorough evaluation of underlying causes and targeted treatments to kick-start the healing process.

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy has gained attention for its role in accelerating tissue repair. When prepared at a high concentration, PRP can pack an extra punch of growth factors and healing proteins into the very spot that needs it most.

What Is PRP?

PRP is a sample of your own blood that's spun down in a centrifuge to concentrate platelets. These platelets release growth factors—natural chemicals your body uses to:

Stimulate cell division

Promote blood vessel formation (angiogenesis)

Reduce inflammation

Recruit stem cells to the injury site

By increasing the concentration of platelets well above baseline levels, you give your body a super-charged dose of its own healing tools.

Key Benefits of High Concentration PRP

Enhanced Collagen ProductionEncourages stronger, more resilient tissue formation in skin, tendons and ligaments.

Medically Approved Next Steps

If you suspect high concentration PRP might help you, here's how to move forward in a medically sound, step-by-step way:

Consult Your Primary Care Provider• Review your overall health, existing conditions and medications.• Get blood work to check for anemia, infection markers and clotting factors.

Discuss Imaging and Diagnostics• X-rays, ultrasound or MRI can pinpoint the exact nature and extent of your injury.• Rule out fractures, significant tendon tears or joint instability that need surgery.

Referral to a PRP-Experienced Specialist• Seek an orthopedic surgeon, sports medicine physician or dermatologist trained in PRP protocols.• Verify they use a high-spin centrifuge system that reliably concentrates platelets 5–7× baseline.

Review the PRP Procedure• The provider will draw a small amount of your blood, process it, then inject the PRP into the target area.• Some clinics combine PRP with ultrasound guidance to ensure precise placement.

Follow-Up and Rehabilitation Plan• Expect at least 1–3 treatments spaced 2–4 weeks apart.• Pair PRP with physical therapy or targeted exercise to maximize functional gains.• Monitor progress—most people notice reduced pain and improved range of motion within 4–6 weeks.

Optimize Your Body's Healing Environment• Eat a balanced diet rich in lean protein, colorful fruits and vegetables.• Stay hydrated and get 7–9 hours of sleep nightly.• Quit smoking and manage stress with meditation or counseling.

When to Seek Immediate Medical Advice

Slow healing can sometimes signal a serious issue. Make sure to speak to a doctor right away if you experience:

Fever or chills around the wound site

Spreading redness or red streaks

Severe, unrelenting pain

Pus or foul-smelling discharge

Numbness, weakness or loss of function
