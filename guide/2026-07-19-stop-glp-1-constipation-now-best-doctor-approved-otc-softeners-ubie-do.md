Understanding GLP-1-Induced Constipation

GLP-1 medications work by slowing gastric emptying and increasing feelings of fullness. While effective for blood sugar control and weight loss, the slower movement through your digestive tract can lead to:

Hard, dry stools

Straining during bowel movements

A sensation of incomplete evacuation

Mild constipation can often be managed at home, but it's important to treat it properly to avoid discomfort and more serious complications.

Key Principles Before You Start

Hydration

Aim for at least 8 cups (about 2 liters) of water daily.

Warm beverages in the morning can help stimulate bowel activity.

Sources: whole grains, fruits, vegetables, psyllium husk.

OTC Stool Softeners and Laxatives

Below are the most commonly recommended OTC products for GLP-1-related constipation. Always follow package instructions and check with your healthcare provider if you have any chronic medical conditions.

1. Stool Softeners (Emollient Laxatives)

Docusate sodium (Colace®)• Dose: 100–200 mg once or twice daily• Onset: 12–72 hours• How it works: Draws water into stool to soften consistency• Pros: Gentle, can be used long term under doctor guidance

2. Osmotic Laxatives

Polyethylene glycol 3350 (MiraLAX®)• Dose: 17 grams (one capful) dissolved in 8 oz of liquid once daily• Onset: 1–3 days• How it works: Retains water in the stool, increasing stool frequency• Pros: Tasteless, generally well tolerated

Magnesium hydroxide (Milk of Magnesia)• Dose: 2–4 tablespoons at bedtime (adults)• Onset: 30 minutes–6 hours• How it works: Attracts water into intestines; increases peristalsis

3. Bulk-Forming Fiber

Psyllium husk (Metamucil®)• Dose: 1 rounded teaspoon in 8 oz liquid up to three times daily• Onset: 12–72 hours• How it works: Absorbs water, softening stool and increasing bulk

Methylcellulose (Citrucel®)• Dose: 1–2 caplets or 1 tablespoon powder once or twice daily• Onset: 12–72 hours

How to Choose the Right Option

Start with a stool softener (docusate) plus increased fluids and fiber.

Add an osmotic laxative like polyethylene glycol if stool softener alone isn't enough.

A saline laxative (milk of magnesia) can work faster but should be used sparingly.

Bulk-forming fiber and stool softeners are safest for ongoing use under supervision.

Safety Tips and Precautions

Avoid Overuse: Chronic stimulant laxative use (senna, bisacodyl) can lead to dependency.

Monitor Electrolytes: Prolonged use of saline laxatives or high-dose osmotics can alter sodium, potassium, and magnesium levels.

Interactions: Some medications (e.g., certain antibiotics, thyroid pills) may bind to mineral oil—take them at different times.

Health Conditions: If you have kidney disease, heart failure, or inflammatory bowel disease, check with your doctor before using magnesium-based products.
