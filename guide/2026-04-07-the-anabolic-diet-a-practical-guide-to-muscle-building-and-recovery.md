What is the Anabolic Diet?

Dr. Mauro DiPasquale developed this plan in 1995 as a metabolic hack that uses alternating fat-burning and glycogen-replenishing phases to mimic anabolic steroid-like hormonal advantages while avoiding the legal consequences and side effects.

The system cuts through complexity: 5 days low-carb, 2 days high-carb. The alternating carbohydrate intake pattern forces your system to alter its metabolism and change its operating system.

Your system converts into a fat-burning machine when you keep your daily carbs below 30g. Testosterone and growth hormone spike naturally. Your physique transforms without requiring the agony of being calorie restrictive.

Then you have the weekend reload, which includes specific higher carb refeeds that stops your metabolism from dropping and combats any progress stagnation. Now, what really separates this approach from all the others you’ve tried in the past?

Principles of the Anabolic Diet

Most guys just focus on macros and not nutrients which can end up ultimately working against their biology. These principles are completely congruent to enhance your physiology, not work against it. The program consists of four core principles, which together create a devastatingly effective system:

Strategic Alternating Carbohydrate Intake

Five consecutive low-carb days with less than 30 g followed by two high carb days. The pattern prevents adaptation while delivering benefits that single-approach plans can’t match.

Training periodization works similarly because you don’t max out every day, right? Your metabolism requires strategic variation in a similar way.

High Fat as Primary Fuel

During low-carb days, you should favor fat for 55-60% of calories, while nutrition should make up 35-40%. Saturated fats are not your enemy, they are essential for optimal hormone production. Animal foods with fatty cuts, whole eggs, and full-fat dairy products transform into performance tools instead of forbidden foods.

Think about it from a psychological perspective. That bland, dried out piece of chicken, you get to trade that out with a juicy New York steak. Also, you get to eat that steak knowing that it comes with improved testosterone production, better satiation, and that bump in dopamine from eating something tasty, which leads to less cortisol.

Protein Preservation

The preservation of tissue during low-carb phases requires sufficient consumption. Aim for a higher amount of 1-1.5g per pound of lean body tissue.

The majority of people follow Instagram influencer advice when it comes to intake, but athletes who follow low-carb approaches need to consume 1.5g per pound according to research for optimal preservation.

Strategic Refeeding

The carb refeed days are not cheat days, they are specific metabolic interventions that help to replenish glycogen stores and reset hormones that can downregulate during prolonged restriction.

Implementing the Anabolic Diet

Most people fall apart in the first 72 hours. You get all excited for something new, but then your system resists the changes, cravings and fatigue eventually get the better of you. This phased approach gradually shifts your system without the brutal break-in period.

Maintenance Phase

Start with a 4-week acclimation period. The maintenance calories should be calculated by multiplying weight in pounds by 18, and this value should be kept constant during the macronutrient adaptation process.

The initial four weeks proved essential to train my system to become more efficient at fat burning without experiencing the metabolic shutdown typical of most programs. The initial week brought some challenges, but my energy levels during week three became more consistent than during previous methods that included moderate to high carbohydrate consumption and the monotonous repetition of similar foods every day.

Bulking Phase

After completing the maintenance phase, now it’s time to increase calories. Take your goal weight and multiply by 15 to determine daily caloric intake. Aim for 1-2 pounds of quality gains every week. Keep on with this every week until you hit your goal weight, or if you notice you’re getting too fluffy in the mirror.

The most common mistake I see people make during a massing phase is jumping the gun by adding calories too fast. Instead of jumping straight to your target intake, try increasing first by just 200 calories per week. Check progress in the mirror every day at the same time. This will be more reliable than just looking at scale weight. If the lines start to blur too much, you’ve likely gone too far too quickly.

Cutting Phase

Now it’s time to reveal that hard-earned growth with a strategic deficit. Aim for 500-700 calories below maintenance. It’s super important to keep consumption high during this phase to preserve muscle gains.

Your weekly fat reduction should not exceed 1.5 pounds. Anymore than this and you could risk breakdown. This strategy enables you to lose weight without losing those precious gains that required numerous months to develop.

FAQs

Will I lose strength during the low-carb phase of the Anabolic Diet?

The initial strength decrease is normal during adaptation. Performance usually stabilizes or improves after 2-3 weeks, especially with weekend carb-ups.

How effective is the Anabolic Diet compared to a standard ketogenic diet?

The program prevents metabolic downregulation through strategic carb cycling. This preserves thyroid function and glycogen that strict keto diets can compromise. Studies with rats have also shown similar benefits.

Can women follow the Anabolic Diet effectively?

Yes, with modified ratios. Women tend to do better with a bit more carbs (50g vs 30g) and 4 low carb days followed by 3 high carb days.

How does aging affect implementation of the approach?

Older individuals require more nutrition and moderate carb refeeds. The plan’s hormone optimization benefits can be particularly valuable for countering age-related decline.
