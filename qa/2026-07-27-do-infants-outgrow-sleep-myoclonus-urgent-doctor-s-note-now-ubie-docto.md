Explanation

Sleep myoclonus in infants is generally considered a benign condition, meaning it is not harmful and does not lead to other problems. It is characterized by sudden muscle jerks that occur during sleep. According to the reference provided, this condition is often confused with epileptic seizures, but it is not related to epilepsy.

Key Points:

Resolution: Infants typically outgrow sleep myoclonus as they get older. The condition usually resolves without any medical intervention.

Nature: It is important to note that these muscle jerks are not dangerous and do not affect the infant's development.

Comparison: While it may look similar to more serious conditions like epilepsy, sleep myoclonus is harmless.

Parents and caregivers generally do not need to worry about sleep myoclonus, but if they have concerns or notice unusual symptoms, using a free AI-powered symptom checker can help determine whether professional medical evaluation is needed.
