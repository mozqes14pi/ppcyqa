Numbness in the lips, also known as lip paresthesia, is a loss of sensation that can feel tingly, prickly, or completely absent. Common causes include dental procedures, allergic reactions, vitamin deficiencies (such as B12), nerve damage, low blood sugar, or in more serious cases, stroke or multiple sclerosis. Persistent or sudden lip numbness—especially when paired with facial drooping, difficulty speaking, or weakness—warrants immediate medical attention.

Dental Issues: Problems with teeth or gums (like an infection) can sometimes lead to numbness in the lips.

Nerve Problems: Conditions, surgeries, or dental procedures affecting nerves can cause lip numbness.

Other Medical Conditions: Persistent, worsening numbness in the lips could be linked to other health problems, which would need further investigation by a doctor.

If lip numbness only happens once in a while and improves without any treatment, it might be worth trying to identify if certain activities or exposures are related to the tingling. For example, causes of temporary lip numbness include cosmetic lip injections or eating some types of spicy food, but lip numbness caused by lip injections or spicy food typically will improve without any treatment.

It's important to pay attention to lip numbness, especially if it gets worse or doesn't go away.
