What are the common causes of irregular menstruation in PCOS and what treatments are available?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Irregular periods in PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome) are typically caused by hormonal imbalances, particularly elevated androgens and insulin resistance, which disrupt ovulation. Common treatments include lifestyle changes like a balanced diet and regular exercise, along with medications such as hormonal birth control, metformin, or ovulation-inducing drugs to help regulate cycles.

Because PCOS symptoms overlap with other conditions—like thyroid disorders or endometriosis—getting clarity early matters. Irregular menstruation in PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) is commonly linked to hormonal imbalances, particularly involving androgens, which are male hormones present in both men and women. This condition can lead to infrequent or irregular menses.

Causes:

Hormonal Imbalance: High levels of androgens can interfere with the normal menstrual cycle, causing irregular periods.

Insulin Resistance: Some women with PCOS have insulin resistance, which can also affect hormone levels and menstrual cycles.

Treatments:

Lifestyle Changes: Maintaining a healthy weight through diet and exercise can help regulate menstrual cycles.

Medications: Doctors might prescribe birth control pills to regulate periods or medications like metformin to improve insulin sensitivity.
