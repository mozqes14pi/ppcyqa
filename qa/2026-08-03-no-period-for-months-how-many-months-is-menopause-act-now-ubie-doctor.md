How many months without a period is considered a sign of menopause?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Menopause is officially defined as 12 consecutive months without a menstrual period, marking the end of reproductive years. It typically occurs between ages 45 and 55, though timing varies. Common signs include hot flashes, night sweats, mood shifts, sleep disturbances, and vaginal dryness caused by declining estrogen levels.

Explanation

Doctors consider a woman to be in menopause when she hasn't had a period for 12 months straight. This time frame is used because it shows that her body has naturally stopped the monthly cycle due to changes in hormone levels that come with age.

While many women may notice changes such as irregular periods or even long gaps between cycles during the perimenopause stage (the transition period), the official sign of menopause is reaching a full year without a period. This period of no bleeding is important because it tells doctors that the changes in your hormones have settled, and your body is moving into a new phase of life.

Even if you have gone several months without a period, if it is less than 12 months, it might not yet indicate full-blown menopause.
