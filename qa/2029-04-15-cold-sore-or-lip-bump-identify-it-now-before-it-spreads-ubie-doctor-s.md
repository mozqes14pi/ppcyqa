How can you tell if the bumps on your lips are cold sores or something else?

Cold sores are small, fluid-filled blisters most often caused by the herpes simplex virus (HSV-1). They typically appear on or around the lips and can be triggered by events like dental procedures, tattoos, stress, illness, or sun exposure. Not every mouth sore is a cold sore, however. Similar-looking lesions can result from other causes, including canker sores, allergic reactions, or irritation linked to drug use, and are not connected to the herpes virus.

Explanation

Cold sores are small, painful blisters that often appear on the lips. They are usually caused by the herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1). According to the references, cold sores can sometimes be triggered by events such as dental procedures or getting a tattoo. These activities may activate the virus, causing the sores to appear.

However, not all lip bumps are cold sores. Other types of sores may occur due to different reasons. For instance, the use of substances like crack cocaine can also lead to the development of sores in the mouth area. These sores are not related to the herpes virus and are caused by the irritating effects of the substance.
