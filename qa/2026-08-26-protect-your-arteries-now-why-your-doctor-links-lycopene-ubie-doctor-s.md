Why Your Doctor Explains the Link Between Lycopene and Arteries

Many people have heard that certain nutrients can help protect the heart, but you might wonder exactly how this works. Lycopene—a natural pigment found in red fruits and vegetables—has drawn attention for its potential role in cardiovascular health. Here's what doctors want you to know about lycopene and heart disease, how it affects your arteries, and practical steps you can take.

What Is Lycopene?Lycopene is a carotenoid, a type of antioxidant that gives tomatoes, watermelon, pink grapefruit and guava their red hue. Unlike some vitamins, lycopene isn't produced by your body; you get it entirely from food. As an antioxidant, lycopene helps neutralize free radicals—unstable molecules that can damage cells and contribute to chronic diseases, including heart disease.

How Lycopene Works in the BodyDoctors explain that lycopene may support artery health through several mechanisms:

Strengthening blood vessel walls

Reducing oxidative stress that leads to inflammation

Improving endothelial function (the layer of cells lining your arteries)

Limiting low-density lipoprotein (LDL) oxidation, a step linked to plaque formation

By targeting these processes, lycopene can help maintain smoother blood flow and reduce the chance of blockages that lead to heart attack or stroke.

The Connection Between Lycopene and Heart DiseaseHeart disease often starts with damage to the inner lining of the arteries. Over time, fats and cholesterol collect, forming plaques that narrow vessels. This process, known as atherosclerosis, raises the risk of clots and reduced blood flow. Research suggests:

Higher blood levels of lycopene are linked to lower markers of inflammation.

People with more lycopene in their diets tend to have fewer signs of arterial stiffness.

Populations consuming diets rich in tomatoes show reduced rates of heart disease.

While lycopene isn't a magic bullet, it's a helpful piece in the larger puzzle of cardiovascular prevention.

What Clinical Studies ShowSeveral well-designed studies point to lycopene's benefits:

A 2012 trial found daily tomato extract improved arterial elasticity in middle-aged adults.
