What should I do if I suspect Legionnaires' disease?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Legionnaires' disease is a serious form of pneumonia caused by Legionella bacteria, and early diagnosis is critical to prevent complications like respiratory failure or kidney damage. If you suspect you have it, see a doctor promptly for testing (typically a urine antigen test or sputum culture) and antibiotic treatment. Symptoms often include high fever, cough, shortness of breath, muscle aches, and headaches — but they can mimic other illnesses, which is why professional evaluation matters.

Get Tested: Tests can help confirm if you have Legionnaires' disease. According to research, urine antigen tests and cultures are commonly used.
