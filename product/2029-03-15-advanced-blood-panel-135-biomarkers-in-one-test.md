What is a blood panel?

A blood panel is a single test that measures a wide set of biomarkers across many systems of your body, rather than checking just a few. Superpower's Advanced Blood Panel measures a wide set of biomarkers from one blood draw and urine sample, including hormones, inherited cardiovascular risk markers like Lipoprotein(a), and nutrient status.

What does the Advanced Blood Panel test?

It covers a broad set of biomarkers across cardiovascular, metabolic, hormonal, nutritional, organ, and inflammatory health, including in-depth hormone testing, inherited cardiovascular risk markers like Lipoprotein(a), and nutrient status.

How is this different from a standard blood test?

A standard checkup typically measures around 15 markers. The Advanced Blood Panel goes far deeper, adding hormones, inherited cardiovascular risk markers like Lipoprotein(a), and nutrient status that routine panels skip. It then turns the results into a personalized protocol.

Do I need to fast before the test?

Yes. Fast from food and caffeine for 10 hours before your appointment. Water is fine.

Is there anything I should stop taking before testing?

Stop taking supplements containing Biotin or Vitamin C before your draw, as they can interfere with certain assays. Keep taking prescribed medications unless your clinician advises otherwise.

Albumin/Globulin Ratio

Albumin is a transport protein produced by the liver. Globulins are a type of protein that is made by the liver and the immune system. The amount of albumin and globulins can be affected by a number of things, including infections, kidney or liver disease, or even dietary protein intake.

Method: Derived from FDA-cleared laboratory results. This ratio/index is not an FDA-cleared test. It aids clinician-directed risk assessment and monitoring and is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Inputs: albumin, globulin.

Albumin

Albumin is a protein made by the liver that has many functions in the body, such as maintaining fluid balance, transporting substances including hormones and vitamins, regulating blood pressure, preventing blood clotting, and providing nutrition.

Method: FDA-cleared clinical laboratory assay performed in CLIA-certified, CAP-accredited laboratories. Used to aid clinician-directed evaluation and monitoring. Not a stand-alone diagnosis.

Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)

ALP is an enzyme that is found in various tissues, mainly in the liver and bone.

Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT)

ALT is an enzyme that is mainly found in the liver cells and helps break down proteins and amino acids.

Apolipoprotein B

Apolipoprotein B (ApoB) is a protein that has many functions in the body, such as transporting lipids (fats), regulating cholesterol metabolism, maintaining blood vessel integrity, and modulating immune responses.
