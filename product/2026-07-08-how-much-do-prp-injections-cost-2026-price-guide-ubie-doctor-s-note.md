How Much Do PRP Injections Cost? (2026 Price Guide)

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

PRP (platelet-rich plasma) injection costs typically range from $400 to $2,000 per session, depending on treatment type and provider:

Joint injections: $600–$1,800

Tendon treatments: $500–$1,500

Hair restoration: $400–$1,200

Cosmetic facial PRP: $700–$2,000

Most patients need 2–4 sessions, bringing total treatment packages to $2,000–$6,000+, especially when add-ons like ultrasound guidance are included.

Key factors that affect PRP pricing:

Provider expertise and credentials

Geographic location

Blood processing methods

Insurance coverage (PRP is often considered elective)

Facility and consultation fees

What Is PRP Therapy?

PRP therapy uses a small sample of your own blood. A centrifuge separates platelets—rich in growth factors—into a concentrated solution that your provider injects into the target area. The goal is to reduce inflammation, speed healing and stimulate tissue growth.

Common uses include:

Joint pain (knee, shoulder, hip)

Tendon injuries (Achilles, tennis elbow)

Hair restoration

Facial rejuvenation (fine lines, acne scars)

Key Factors That Affect PRP Injection Cost

Board-certified physicians and experienced sports medicine specialists typically charge more.

Urban centers and coastal areas generally have higher rates than rural regions.

Some conditions require multiple sites or repeat sessions.

Single-spin vs. double-spin centrifugation systems can affect both platelet concentration and price.

Clinic overhead, use of imaging equipment (ultrasound) or private surgical suites may add to your bill.

Is PRP Covered by Insurance?

Cosmetic PRP (facial rejuvenation, hair restoration): Almost never covered.

Orthopedic and sports-medicine PRP: Some insurers may cover a portion if deemed medically necessary—for example, for chronic tendon injuries unresponsive to conservative care.

Always verify with your insurance provider. You may need pre-authorization or documentation of prior treatment failures.

Additional Costs to Consider

When budgeting for PRP, factor in:

Initial consultation (often $100–$300)

Follow-up visits (may be bundled or billed separately)

Imaging (ultrasound or X-ray guidance can add $200–$500 per session)

Physical therapy or supplemental treatments (e.g., laser therapy)

How to Budget for PRP Injections

Research local clinics and compare fee schedules.

Ask about all-inclusive packages vs. à la carte pricing.

Inquire whether imaging and follow-up visits are included.

Explore financing or membership options.

Plan for additional rehab or maintenance sessions that may benefit long-term results.

Preparing for Your Appointment

Bring prior imaging or medical records.

Confirm any pre-procedure requirements (fasting, medication adjustments).

Arrange transportation if you plan to combine PRP with other sedatives or therapies.

Review potential side effects—mild swelling, bruising or discomfort at injection sites are common but usually resolve in days.
