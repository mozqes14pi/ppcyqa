Internal Medicine

Shingles near the eye, known as herpes zoster ophthalmicus (HZO), is a medical urgency that requires prompt antiviral treatment—typically within 72 hours of symptom onset—to prevent vision loss. Unlike shingles on other parts of the body, HZO can affect the cornea, retina, and optic nerve, so treatment often includes specialized eye care alongside oral antivirals like acyclovir or valacyclovir.

Because HZO symptoms (rash near the eye, pain, redness, blurred vision) can overlap with other conditions, and because timing is critical, it's important to identify what's happening as early as possible. Explanation

When shingles occurs near the eye, it's called herpes zoster ophthalmicus. This type of shingles can be more serious because it can affect the eye and cause complications.

Treatment Differences:

Antiviral Medication: Quick treatment with antiviral medicines is very important to help control the virus and reduce the risk of eye problems. These medicines work best if started within 72 hours of the rash appearing.

Eye Care: Sometimes, doctors might prescribe eye drops or other treatments to protect the eye and prevent damage.

Monitoring: People with shingles near the eye need to see a doctor regularly to check for any changes or complications in the eye.
